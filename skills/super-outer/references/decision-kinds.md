# Decision kinds

Classify each decision, not an entire discipline.

## Invariant

A condition must hold: authorization boundaries, data integrity, accessible
semantics, truthful evidence, or safe rollback. Apply it and report the proof;
do not ask permission to be correct.

## Evidence-led trade-off

Several mechanisms are valid and measurable consequences differ. Research and
recommend a mechanism. Propose a router question only when product thresholds
such as acceptable latency, loss, cost, availability, or recovery time remain
unknown.

## Interpretive

The answer depends on the company's product character, user expectation,
content language, or interaction grammar. Inspect explicit and observed local
evidence first. Propose a question only when that evidence cannot resolve the
choice, and never import another company's taste.

A module may return all three kinds in one review. No kind changes a module's
rank, because the twelve modules are peers.

## Confirming a recommended stack

Never ask the user to select frameworks, libraries, queues, caches, databases,
hosting or other mechanisms from scratch. Recommend the simplest safe
mechanism yourself and explain its trade-off plainly.

Confirming a recommended stack is the one stack decision that belongs to the
owner. After the [story walk](story-walk.md), show one recommended stack and
one runner-up. For each, say what it means for customers, the monthly cost at
the brief's scale and at ten times that scale, and how hard it would be to move
away later. The owner accepts the recommendation or picks the runner-up. Never
offer an open choice among tools. Record the answer, with its reason, in the
product brief's Stack section.
