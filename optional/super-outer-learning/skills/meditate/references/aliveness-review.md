# Review the choices in a piece of work

Use this internal review for an artifact or a preference comparison. The questions guide reasoning; they are not required sections in the reader's study.

## Examine observable choices

For each relevant question, identify the passage, interaction, design choice, learner action or stated preference that supports the answer. Distinguish evidence from interpretation.

1. What consequential choice does the work make, and what reasonable alternative was available?
2. Which specific detail attracts attention or creates difficulty? How do hierarchy, pacing or constraints affect its interpretation?
3. What shows contact with the actual task: a supplied constraint, tested interaction, quotation, result or decision? Do not treat visual finish as proof that a claim is true.
4. Which explanation is unnecessary or premature? What can be removed, shortened or demonstrated without losing meaning?
5. Which choices would remain recognisable without a logo or reputation? Do not infer a distinctive approach from branding alone.

Summarise the choice that helps the work and, when supported, the choice that reduces its usefulness. Do not average these questions into a score or return a seven-field review checklist.

## Test an explanation the user offers for a preference

When the user offers an explanation for a preference, treat it as a hypothesis and use it only when the current preference question makes it relevant. Current explicit feedback and approved examples govern output.

Consider an admired example without the proposed characteristics, a disliked example that has them, or another explanation that better accounts for the same choice. Test whether the proposed principle could help make a later decision. If evidence is mixed, keep the idea provisional or narrow it to the relevant kind of work. A useful limit or next test may appear briefly in the application; no separate visible panel is compulsory.

## Respect sensitive context

When the user explicitly supplies a private preference for comparison, restrict the analysis to that evidence and observable similarities or differences. Do not infer causes, diagnoses or intimate facts. Do not generalise about any group of people.
